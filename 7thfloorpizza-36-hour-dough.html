Rule of thumb from the video: only 1g of instant yeast per 1kg of flour. Yield: 6 balls of ~280g, or 3 balls from the half batch.

Yeast works ~1.5–1.7× faster at 26°C. Same 1g yeast, shorter clock: start checking the bulk at 5 hrs and ball it once tripled. Use cold water for the mix.

Prefer the 10-hr bulk at 26°C? Cut the yeast to ~0.6g: dissolve 5g in 500g water, use 60g of that liquid and take 60g off the recipe water.

Key Tips:

  • Mix the yeast into the dry flour before any water goes in, then add the water little by little.
  • The salt goes in only after the first 10 minutes of kneading. The dough will tear apart at first, that is normal.
  • Kneading is done when the dough is smooth again and you can't feel any salt grains under your fingers.
  • Readiness test before adding salt: push the dough and it should push back.
  • Never seal the bulk container airtight. The dough needs to breathe: damp cloth, loose lid, something heavy on top.

Balling & Baking:

  • When tipping out the bulk dough, pull it so the top stays on top.
  • Close each ball completely, either by folding the edges underneath or by rolling it on the bench while tucking the seam.
  • A few drops of oil (or a dusting of flour) in each container stops the balls sticking when you need them.
  • The fridge stage is flexible: 24 hours minimum, but 48, 72 or even 96 hours still bake very well.
  • Take the balls out 2–3 hours before baking. Cold dough resists stretching.

Example schedule (pizza Saturday 7pm):

Friday 6:00am (9:00am at 26°C)

Mix & knead, then bulk at room temp.

Friday 4:00pm

Ball into 6 × 280g, into the fridge.

Saturday 4:00pm (5:00pm at 26°C)

Balls out of the fridge to warm up.

Saturday 7:00pm

Stretch, top, bake 90 seconds.

Main times assume ~20°C, bracketed times ~26°C. Whatever the kitchen, check for the dough tripling rather than the clock. In a warm room the balls also go slack faster, so stretch as soon as they feel soft and no longer cold in the centre.
