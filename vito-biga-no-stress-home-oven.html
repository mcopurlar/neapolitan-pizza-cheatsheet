Vito Iacopelli's Biga No Stress Dough for the Home Oven

100% Biga, 48-Hour Cold Preferment · Ingredients & Timeline Cheat Sheet

Source: How to Make Perfect Biga No Stress Pizza Dough at Home 100% by Vito Iacopelli · method by Roberto Susta (Pizzaiolo Napoletano)

Ingredients (~1.03kg dough, ~67% hydration)

Ingredient Amount Baker's %
The biga (all of the flour goes in here)
"00" flour600g100%
Water300ml50%
Honey (dissolved in the water)5g0.8%
Dry yeast (or 10g fresh)5g0.8%
The dough (added after 48 hours)
Water100ml+17%
Salt15g2.5%
Extra virgin olive oil (for a home oven)1 tbsp~2%

Total water 400ml on 600g flour is ~67%. Vito calls it 70% and says a home-oven dough can go up to 80%. The yeast is high compared with a direct dough because the biga is stiff at 50% and spends 48 hours cold.

Total Timeline ≈ 54 hours

  • ~5 min · Mix the biga (no kneading)
  • 2 hrs · Biga at room temp
  • 48 hrs · Biga in the fridge
  • 1 hr · Biga back at room temp
  • ~30 min · Mix the dough + 15 min rest
  • 1 hr · Bulk, wrapped, room temp
  • 1 hr · Balled, room temp
  • 2 stages · Home oven bake

All the long fermentation happens in the biga. Once it is mixed into dough, only two short room-temp rests remain. The 1-hour rests assume ~20°C and shrink in a warm kitchen.

Biga & Mixing Tips:

  • The biga is not kneaded. Wet the flour into rough lumps and stop. Building gluten at this stage is the mistake, the 48 hours in the fridge do the work.
  • The honey feeds the yeast for a more natural fermentation. Dissolve it in the water first.
  • Keep the lid resting on top, never sealed. The biga needs to breathe.
  • Use a stand mixer for the final dough. A 100% biga is hard to bring together by hand.
  • The dough looks too sticky right after mixing. That is normal: the 15-minute rest in the bowl strengthens the gluten, and a 5-second burst afterwards pulls it cleanly off the hook.
  • A home oven runs cooler than a pizza oven, so the dough is wetter and gets a spoon of oil to stay soft and puffy.

Handling & Home Oven Baking:

  • Handle with oiled hands, not flour, to keep the hydration. Use a little flour only if it is too hard to manage.
  • After the bulk hour, don't press the air out. Cut and ball fast and put the balls well apart in the box.
  • The two 1-hour rests are for ~20°C. In a warmer kitchen they are shorter, so go by the puffiness of the balls.
  • Preheat a baking steel (best) or stone at the oven's maximum for 40 to 60 minutes.
  • Bake in two stages: first the stretched base with sauce until golden, then add mozzarella, a drizzle of oil on the rim for colour, and finish under the broiler until the cheese bubbles.
  • The broiler burns fast. Watch it and switch it off as soon as the rim darkens.

The topping in the video: ragù pizza

Not a Margherita this time. Base of plain cream, then ragù, Romano cheese, and after the first bake fresh mozzarella, olive oil, more ragù and more Romano.

Quick ragù for pizza:

  1. Olive oil, chopped celery, carrot and onion. Cook slowly until golden.
  2. Add minced meat, a pinch of salt and pepper. A splash of white wine is traditional.
  3. Add about half a cup of tomato passata and some basil.
  4. Lid on, lowest heat, 20 to 30 minutes. It should be thick, not saucy, and cooled a little before topping.
