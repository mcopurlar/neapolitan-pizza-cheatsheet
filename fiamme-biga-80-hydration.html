The pizzeria makes 10kg flour + 10g yeast + 5kg water as biga, then adds 10kg flour, another 10g yeast, 11kg cold water and ~500g salt: about 130 balls. The first cold-water pour isn't weighed on camera; 250g is what brings the total to 80% with the 300g added last. Neither the flour nor the yeast type is named in the video.

Total Timeline ≈ 42 hours

  • 2 min · Mix the biga in reverse (no gluten)
  • 16 hrs · Biga at 18°C until doubled
  • ~15 min · Final dough in the mixer, salt last
  • 40–60 min · Rest in the mixer bowl
  • 15–20 min · Fold, then rest
  • 40–60 min · Balled (280g), room temp
  • 24 hrs · Balls in the fridge at ~5°C
  • not shown · Warm up, stretch & bake

Two long stages: the biga ferments 16 hours at a steady 18°C, the balls mature 24 hours cold. Everything in between takes under 3 hours on day 2.

Dashed boxes are not shown in the video. Fiammè's balls go straight from 24 hours in the fridge into the evening service.

Biga & Mixing Tips:

  • The biga stays rough and torn: "Grezzo stracciato deve essere!" Two minutes, then stop. It should ferment and double, not build gluten. If a little gluten forms, tear it apart.
  • Fiammè keeps the biga in a proofing cell at a steady 18°C for 16 hours. At home a cool room, cellar or wine fridge at 17–19°C does the same job.
  • No reverse gear on a stand mixer? Stir the biga briefly with a spoon or the paddle until the flour is just wet, then stop.
  • Use only cold water in the final dough so it doesn't overheat in the mixer. Antonio's note: if your biga fermented in the fridge, you don't need cold water.
  • Add the same yeast dose again in the final dough. "If you put 10g on 10kg, I advise adding another 10g."
  • "Always try to give the water little by little." On a stand mixer, add the 300g in 4–5 pours and let each one absorb. If 80% is new to you, hold back the last 50–100g.
  • Salt goes in after the 7 minutes, then the mixer stops for 2 minutes. Salt isn't only flavour: it makes the dough stronger and easier to work, and Antonio credits the 2-minute pause for the structure.

Resting, Balling & Cold Proof:

  • The finished dough should not be sticky. "The less sticky, the better", which is what the cold water helps with.
  • The 40–60 minute rest in the bowl is fundamental at high hydration: the dough gets stronger and you get balls with strength. Afterwards it shows bubbles on top.
  • One fold on the bench, then 15–20 minutes rest before cutting. The fold gives the final ball even more strength.
  • Ball at 280g. Wet or lightly oiled hands help with an 80% dough.
  • Leave the balls 40–60 minutes at room temperature, then 24 hours at about 5°C. They are ready for the next evening.
  • 80% is far wetter than the classic Neapolitan rules (55.5–62.5%), and the biga plus a 24-hour cold proof is a contemporary method. Fiammè calls it "authentic taste with a contemporary method".

Weighing 0.5g or 0.25g of yeast:

Most kitchen scales can't. Dissolve 1g fresh yeast in 100g cold water and use the solution:

  • 1kg flour: 50g solution = 0.5g yeast. Use it as part of the water: 50g solution + 200g water in the biga, and again 50g solution + 200g water for the first cold-water pour.
  • 500g flour: 25g solution = 0.25g yeast, with 100g water in each of the same two places.

The video doesn't say which yeast; Italian pizzerias normally mean fresh. With instant dry yeast use about a third (~0.17g per dose for 1kg), easiest with the same solution trick.

What the video doesn't say:

  • Flour. Not named. A 16-hour biga at 80% total hydration needs a strong flour; a 00 or 0 around W300 or above is the usual choice.
  • Yeast type. See the left. The amount is tiny either way: 0.1% of the flour in total.
  • Warming up and baking. The video ends with the balls going into service. At home, take them out of the fridge a few hours ahead and go by the dough: soft, relaxed and puffy.

Example schedule (pizza Sunday 7pm):

Friday 8:00pm

Mix the biga, 2 minutes. Into a cool spot at ~18°C.

Saturday 12:00pm

Final dough: torn biga, cold water, yeast, flour, the rest of the water, salt.

Saturday 12:15pm

Rest 40–60 min in the bowl, then fold and rest 15–20 min.

Saturday ~1:45pm

Ball into 280g. Leave 40–60 min on the counter.

Saturday ~3:00pm

Balls into the fridge at ~5°C for 24 hours.

Sunday 3:00–5:00pm

Balls out of the fridge to warm up. Pizza at 7pm.

The biga timing is the one to respect: 16 hours at 18°C. If your spot is warmer, the biga doubles sooner, so mix the final dough when it has doubled rather than by the clock.
