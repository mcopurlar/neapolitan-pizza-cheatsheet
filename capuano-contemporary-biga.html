The on-screen cards: biga = 1kg Nuvola Super, 500ml water, 3g dry yeast; final dough "for 10 pizzas of 300g" = the biga, 1kg Nuvola, "1l water (70%)", 60g salt. All of that water is 1.5l on 2kg flour, which is 75%, not 70%. For the labelled 70%, leave out the last 100ml. The water goes in little by little anyway, so let the dough decide. The ingredients weigh ~3.5kg, so you get 11 balls of 300g rather than 10. Capuano gives the yeast as 1% fresh or 0.3% dry of the biga flour; no more yeast goes in on day 2. 60g salt on 2kg flour is 3%, at the high end (most sheets here use 2.5%).

Total Timeline ≈ 24 hours

  • 3–4 min · Mix the biga in reverse (no gluten)
  • 16–18 hrs · Biga at 18°C
  • ~15 min · Final dough in the mixer, salt last
  • 15 min · Rest, then one fold (giro di corda)
  • 1 hr · Bulk in a tub, room temp
  • until 2× · Balled (280–300g), room temp
  • 4–6 hrs · Fridge at 4°C holds the risen balls
  • not stated · Stretch, top, wood-fired bake

One long stage: the biga, 16–18 hours at a steady 18°C. Day 2 is short: about 1.5 hours to balls, then they rise at room temperature until doubled. The fridge doesn't ferment the dough here, it holds ready balls for service.

Biga & Mixing Tips:

  • The biga needs a strong flour, "always with W above 300". Capuano uses Caputo Nuvola Super for the biga and the lower-W Caputo Nuvola for the final dough.
  • Biga formula: 50% water and 1% fresh or 0.3% dry yeast on the biga flour. Dissolve the dry yeast in the water first.
  • Run the spiral mixer in reverse and pour the water in while it turns. Don't let a gluten network form: 3–4 minutes, no more. It must look rough ("grezzo"), not kneaded.
  • No reverse gear? The video's tip: run the mixer on first speed for 3–4 minutes, or mix completely by hand.
  • The biga ferments 16–18 hours at 18°C. At home a cool room, cellar or wine fridge at 17–19°C does the job.
  • Day 2 order: water in the bowl first, then the flour, then the biga. The dough keeps absorbing water, so add the rest little by little.
  • Salt goes in with the last bit of water so it dissolves.
  • The bowl should stay clean when the dough pulls away from it. That means the gluten network is well formed.

Resting, Balling & Stretching:

  • Rest 15 minutes, then a giro di corda: fold the dough over itself to tighten it, lift it out and into a tub.
  • After 1 hour at room temperature, cut and ball at 280–300g.
  • Put the balls on a tray and wait until they double in volume.
  • Capuano then keeps them in the fridge at 4°C, which lets him use them over the next 4–6 hours. In the video the balls rose at room temperature and spent about 30 minutes in the fridge.
  • Stretching: push all the gas from the centre out to the rim, never pressing the cornicione, then widen the disc. That trapped gas is what gives the tall, airy rim of the contemporary style.
  • Bake on a perforated peel ("one of the best") in a wood-fired oven.

The pizza in the video: tomato & provola

  • Crush the tomatoes by hand or with a food mill, never with a blender.
  • Season them with 1g salt for every 100g of tomato.
  • Cut the provola ahead of time and leave it in the fridge to dry out, so it doesn't release water on the pizza.

Weighing the yeast: 1.5g dry yeast is about ½ tsp, 0.75g about ¼ tsp. Or dissolve 3g in 100ml of the biga water and use 50ml (1kg flour) or 25ml (500g flour) of it as part of that water.

What the video doesn't say:

  • Mixing time and speed for the final dough. Go by the bowl coming away clean.
  • How long the balls take to double. It depends on your kitchen. Go by the volume, and use the fridge to hold balls that are ready early.
  • Bake time and oven temperature. It is a wood-fired oven; contemporary Neapolitan pizza usually bakes in 60–90 seconds at 430–480°C.
  • Other flours. Without Nuvola Super, use any 0 or 00 flour rated W 300 or above for the biga.

Example schedule (pizza Saturday 7pm):

Friday 9:00pm

Mix the biga, 3–4 minutes. Into a cool spot at ~18°C.

Saturday 2:00pm

Final dough: water, flour, biga, the rest of the water little by little, salt with the last of it.

Saturday ~2:30pm

Rest 15 min, giro di corda, into a tub for 1 hour.

Saturday ~3:45pm

Cut and ball at 280–300g. Onto a tray, covered, at room temperature.

Once doubled

Into the fridge at 4°C if you're not ready. They hold for 4–6 hours.

Saturday 7:00pm

Stretch without touching the rim, tomato, provola, bake.

The biga timing is the one to respect: 16–18 hours at 18°C. If your spot is warmer, the biga is ready sooner. The balls usually take a few hours to double: in a warm kitchen they're ready early and wait in the fridge, in a cold one move day 2 an hour earlier.
